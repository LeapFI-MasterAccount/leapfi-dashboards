The pipeline

From feed to filed obligation

Every source lands in the same five layer pipe, whether it exposes a JSON API or only an RSS headline. The first stages run unattended around the clock. The database holds each item as unreviewed until a qualified person confirms it. Only at that point does it become authoritative and eligible to shape a recommendation.

Obligation database

A bitemporal, append only, provenance anchored store. This is the risk engine system of record.

◤ FULLY AUTOMATED: collect, extract, normalize, store, diff, re-flag
◤ HUMAN GATE: confirm, approve, publish

A reviewer works a confidence ranked queue rather than a firehose. Diff based reprocessing sends only what actually changed back into that queue, so the human effort scales with the pace of regulatory change and not with the size of the corpus.

Inside the risk engine

The obligation database

Every fetched item becomes one or more obligation records on the canonical spine. This is the object the engine already scores, gap analyzes, and reports against, so the regulatory feeds simply keep it fed and current. Records are append only and bitemporal, which lets a reviewer or an examiner ask both what a rule required on a given date and what the platform believed on that same date.

One record, authority agnostic, mapped to a topic node such as BSA and AML, UDAAP, fair lending, information security, third party risk, model risk, privacy, or AI governance
provenance
issuing body, instrument type, exact pin-cite, verbatim span, and content hash
statement and modality
normalized text, and one of mandatory, prohibition, permission, or conditional
actor and action
who must act, the action primitive, and the trigger
applicability
a declarative predicate over charter, asset threshold, and activity, tested against the institution profile
dates
effective, compliance, sunset, and the recorded-at system time
crosswalk edge
a typed link to a spine node, one of equal, subset, superset, or intersects
lifecycle status
proposed, final, stayed, or superseded, held as first-class data and never hard-coded
extraction meta
method, model or version, and confidence score
review state
unreviewed, then approved, with the reviewer and the proposal versus approved diff
Staying on top of it

The always-on monitoring loop

The loop is what turns a one time load into a living database. Cheap change detection runs constantly, and the expensive work fires only when something has actually changed.

Poll a cheap check, titles.json or an RSS timestamp
→
Detect change has the amendment stamp advanced
→
Pull the diff only the changed slice
→
Re-flow extract, normalize, crosswalk
→
Flag edges dependent obligations to re-review
→
Raise event this changed, here is what it touches
Cadence

Tiered polling

Volatile, high impact sources sit in fast lanes, and stable codified text sits in slower ones. An RSS item is caught within its publish cycle, and eCFR is checked against its amendment stamp.

Efficiency

Diff based reprocessing

Only changed provisions re-enter the pipe. A single CFR amendment does not re-run the whole corpus. It re-flows one slice and re-flags only the crosswalk edges that touch it.

Defensibility

Source against interpretation

A change in the law and a change in our own mapping are recorded as separate events, so a re-map never reads to an examiner as a legal change.

Where the person sits

Automated to the edge of judgment

You asked to automate the gathering and keep people on review, and that line falls exactly where the liability does. Everything up to a legal interpretation claim is machine work, and the claim itself is confirmed by a person.

The machine does

Collect and prepare

  • Watches every API and RSS source continuously
  • Detects changes and pulls the full text with a hash
  • Drafts atomic obligations with a confidence score
  • Proposes crosswalk edges and applicability
  • Ranks the review queue and re-flags on change
The person does

Confirm and authorize

  • Approves an obligation as authoritative
  • Confirms the crosswalk and applicability call
  • Signs off on a redline before it routes
  • Holds the final word on citations
Only then

It reaches the customer

  • Gap analysis runs on approved obligations only
  • Recommendations cite verbatim, reviewed sources
  • The MCP and API serve current, approved policy
  • A full audit trail stands behind every claim
The bottom line

Yes, the entire gather can be automated. A mix of REST APIs and RSS feeds covers every federal banking and credit union governing body. The APIs carry the codified backbone along with the FDIC, CFPB, SEC, and OFAC data. The RSS and subscription feeds carry the guidance layer from the OCC, the Federal Reserve, the NCUA, and FinCEN. LegiScan and Open States cover state legislation. The one honest gap is state regulator guidance from bodies such as NYDFS and NMLS, which stays part scrape. All of it flows into a single obligation database in the risk engine, kept current by the monitoring loop, and none of it crosses into a customer recommendation until a person has approved it.
